Power and efficiency

Where the pressure goes

Euler pressure is the ideal ρ·u₂·cu2 after slip. Everything else is subtracted from it; the outlet dynamic pressure is what free-air delivery throws away at the 60 mm deep outlet.

Checks

Motor

Dimensions for SolidWorks origin at the bottom-left-back corner, x across, y up, z toward the front (inlets at z = depth)

Scroll wall: equation-driven curve in polar form about each wheel centre, r(θ) = r₀·ek(θ−θ₀) for θ₀ ≤ θ ≤ 2π (θ counter-clockwise from +x seen from the front, left fan). The right fan is the mirror image about x = L/2 and turns clockwise.

Blades: camber line with blade angle varying linearly from β₁ to β₂ (measured from the tangent, >90° = forward-curved); a single circular arc between the same end angles is close enough for CFD.

Model basis and accuracy

Impeller: Euler work with Wiesner slip (scaled ×2 for short sirocco blades), blade blockage, 90 % volumetric efficiency for tongue and inlet-gap leakage. Losses: inlet nozzle, inlet incidence on the blade (sirocco blades always run with large incidence), passage friction, scroll mixing of the wheel exit jet down to the throat velocity, scroll friction, diffuser at 60 % recovery, disc friction. Coefficients are set so the results sit in the range of published sirocco data (peak static pressure coefficient about 1.5–1.7, peak total efficiency about 0.6–0.7).

Calibration: SolidWorks Flow Simulation of the default geometry at 130 rad/s (1241 rpm) in free air gave 354 m³/h; the uncalibrated model said 494 m³/h. The scroll friction coefficient was raised from 0.20 to 1.00, which puts the model about 3 % below that CFD point (343 m³/h), so it now errs on the cautious side. The green diamond on the pressure chart is the CFD point scaled to the current speed with the fan laws. The scroll is space-limited (spiral angle about 2.9° against roughly 4° ideal), which is where the extra loss comes from. One point fixes the free-air flow only: a second CFD point with outlet back-pressure, plus impeller torque goals, would fix the curve slope and the shaft power.
