How to use Undertow

How to play Undertow: the panel, the nine engines, harmony, effects, tides, the autopilot, scenes, and five recipes to start from.

Undertow is a four-voice cinematic drone machine that can play itself: press START, raise AUTOPILOT, and it keeps evolving. Everything else in this guide is about shaping what it does.

Quick start

Six steps take you from silence to a drone that plays itself.

  1. Press START. Browsers only allow sound after a tap or click, so nothing plays until you do. The default patch fades in: a D minor wall with TITAN on D1, WALL on D2, BRASS on F3 and ATMOS wind on top.
  2. Turn VOLUME (the small knob bottom right of the AUTOPILOT panel) to a comfortable level. Headphones or a speaker with real bass help: half of this machine lives below 100 Hz.
  3. Tap the voice keys A–D to open each voice's page. Knob 1 picks the engine; knobs 2 and 3 are that engine's character controls.
  4. Turn the big AUTOPILOT dial to about 50%. Within a minute the harmonic centre moves, voices breathe in and out, and slow surges swell the whole mix.
  5. Press NUDGE whenever you want the next autopilot move now instead of waiting.
  6. When you like what you hear, open PATCH, press STORE and tap a scene slot to keep it.

To play by hand instead, keep AUTOPILOT below 5%: nothing moves unless you move it.

How the sound flows

Each voice's LEVEL sets its dry signal, and its SPACE knob sets how much also feeds the shared effects bus. Because the cloud, delay and plate are shared, one REVERB setting serves all four voices.

The panel and the screen

The page is laid out like the hardware it previews: a screen, a macro panel, four voice keys, a row of page keys and one bank of 8 knobs whose meaning changes with the page.

Reading a knob

The coloured arc is your setting. A small yellow dot on the ring is what you actually hear right now, after autopilot drift and tides move it. When the dot is far from the arc, the machine is wandering; lock the knob to pin it to your setting.

Reading a voice tile

The status line tells you what the voice is doing:

  • READY: the engine is off; press START.
  • SWELLING IN: fading up over the SWELL time.
  • SOUNDING: at full level; the line also shows its frequency.
  • BREATHING OUT / RESTING · AUTOPILOT: the autopilot has faded it out for now.
  • FADING TO SWAP ENGINE: it will come back as the engine named after the arrow.
  • OFF: you switched it off.

Reading the screen

  • Left: spectrum. 20 Hz to 16 kHz, with vertical lines at every C (C1–C9). It is tilted +4.5 dB per octave so high partials stay visible. Each voice is a coloured line labelled with its note.
  • Right: harmony ring. One turn is one octave, starting from the root at the top. White dots are the scale's notes, labelled with their ratios. Gold wedges are the chord tones around the current centre; the brightest is the centre itself. Coloured dots are the voices: angle is pitch, distance from the middle is octave.
  • Ring halo. A gold halo means a surge is swelling; a blue one means an ebb is darkening the mix.
  • Top left: key, scale, tuning and current centre. Top right: autopilot level, then surge/morph progress or the active grain count, the output peak in dB, and CLOUD FROZEN when the cloud is held.
  • Bottom: the last three autopilot decisions, newest on top (hidden on phones; the full log is on the AUTOPILOT page).

Voices and engines

The four voices are identical: each can run any of the nine engines, and each has the same 8-knob page. A is pan-centred, B leans left, C leans right, D sits in the centre; most engines are stereo on their own.

The voice page

Knob Name What it does Range
1 ENGINE Picks the sound source. Switching crossfades in about 0.2 s. 9 engines
2 CHAR 1 First character control; its name changes with the engine (table below). 0–100%
3 CHAR 2 Second character control. 0–100%
4 TONE Cutoff of the voice's 24 dB ladder low-pass. Below the note's own pitch the voice goes almost silent. 30 Hz–16 kHz
5 LEVEL Voice volume (squared curve, so the top half does most of the work). 0–100%
6 SPACE Send to the cloud, delay and reverb. 0% = completely dry. 0–100%
7 DEGREE Which note of the scale, counted from the centre. ↑ and ↓ mark degrees an octave above or below. 7 down to 14 up
8 OCTAVE Register: C1 range is the lowest (about 33–65 Hz). C1–C6 range

The engine picker under the bank does the same as knob 1, with a one-line description. RE-ROLL VOICE gives this voice a random engine and settings suited to its role, keeping its note.

The nine engines

Engine Sound world CHAR 1 CHAR 2 Works best on
WALL Solar 42-style walls of sound DETUNE: spread of seven drifting saws; above 55% adds a fifth RESONANCE: ladder resonance, sings near 100% Any voice; B by default
BRASS Blade Runner brass pads BLOOM: how far and fast the filter opens on each swell or note change ENSEMBLE: layer detune and delayed vibrato depth B or C, mid register
TITAN Dune-style weight DRIVE: saturation of the saw stack (the sub stays clean) THROAT: a whistling overtone, sweeping harmonics 5–14 A, lowest octaves
CHOIR Vocal pads VOWEL: U, O, A, E, I (dark to bright) ENSEMBLE: detune, vibrato and breath B or C; low octaves for chant
ORGAN Cathedral pipe organ REGISTRATION: flute 8', soft, principal, plenum, tutti with 32' AIR: wind noise and beating between ranks A or B
METAL Bowed resonant hulls STRUCTURE: string, bar, membrane, bell DECAY: ring time, 0.3 s to about 27 s C or D
VOID Alien ship hum RATIO: ring-mod ratio 0.5:1 to 4.6:1; odd values sound inharmonic METAL: comb feedback and noise; high = clangy A for hum, D for texture
SHEPARD Endless rise or fall MOTION: centre = still, left = falling, right = rising REGISTER: where the cluster of octaves sits C or D
ATMOS Wind and rumble COLOR: rumble, wind, hiss GUST: gust strength and distant crackle D, upper octaves

All nine are level-matched within about 5 dB, so swapping engines does not jump in volume.

Harmony

Every voice is tuned as a scale degree above one root, so the four voices always stay in the same key, however the autopilot moves them.

Knob Name What it does Range
1 ROOT The key's tonic. C to B
2 SCALE Which notes are allowed (list below). 12 scales
3 TUNING JUST uses pure frequency ratios (beatless, glowing). EQUAL rounds them to piano tuning, for playing along with other instruments. JUST / EQUAL
4 CENTRE Shifts every voice up or down the scale together, keeping their spacing. The autopilot moves this when HARMONY is on. 7 steps down to 7 up
5 GLIDE How slowly a voice slides to a new note. 0.05–20 s
6 SWELL Fade-in time when a voice starts; fade-out is 1.3× longer. 0.05–40 s
7 DRIFT Analog-style wander of every voice's pitch (up to about ±9 cents) and level. 0–100%
8 — Free slot, reserved for a hardware SHIFT function. —

Scales

Scale Character Notes

PENTA MAJ / PENTA MIN Open, impossible to clash 5

DORIAN Minor with a lifted 6th; Blade Runner territory 7

The HARMONY page lists the selected scale's ratios and cents under the bank, and the screen's ring shows them as dots.

How notes move

A voice's note = ROOT + its DEGREE + CENTRE, in its OCTAVE. Change one voice's DEGREE to move only that voice; change CENTRE to move the whole chord. With AUTOPILOT at 25% or more, the centre walks around the scale by steps chosen for each scale (mostly fourths and fifths, never more than 5 steps from your setting). From 50% the voices also re-voice onto the chord tones around the new centre, which keeps every move consonant.

Effects: SPACE and COLOUR

Each voice's SPACE knob feeds one shared send bus, which runs through the cloud, then the delay, then the plate reverb. The COLOUR page shapes the reverb's tail and the master output.

SPACE page

Knob Name What it does Range
1 CLOUD How much of the send is replaced by grains, plus some grain feedback. 0–100%
2 GRAIN Length of each grain. Short grains shimmer and sparkle; long ones smear into slow washes. The number of grains adjusts automatically. 40 ms–1.4 s
3 SCATTER Randomness: how far back in the last 5 s grains read, and how irregular their timing and pitch are. Above 50% timing becomes fully random. 0–100%
4 GRAIN PITCH Transposes every grain. RND OCT picks −12, 0, +12 or +19 per grain. −24 to +24 semitones, RND OCT
5 DELAY Ping-pong echo level and feedback together. 0–100%
6 TIME Echo time. Changes glide like tape, so turning it bends the repeats. 40 ms–2.5 s
7 REVERB Plate reverb level on the send. 0–100%
8 DECAY Tail length and room size. Above 97% the tail never dies. about 1 s to endless

FREEZE (page key, or F on a keyboard) stops the cloud recording, so grains keep reading the same five seconds while everything else carries on. The autopilot can also freeze the cloud for 6–30 s at a time.

COLOUR page

Knob Name What it does Range
1 SHIMMER Octave-up pitch shift inside the reverb's loop: tails rise and glitter. 0–100%
2 DAMPING Darkens reverb tails and delay repeats. High = distant and muffled. 0–100%
3 WOW Slow tape pitch wobble plus faster flutter on the whole mix. 0–100%
4 DRIVE Tape-style saturation on the master. 0–100%
5 AGE Tape hiss and loss of high frequencies. 0–100%
6 TILT Tilts the whole mix around 700 Hz: left darker, right brighter. −6 to +6 dB
7–8 — Free slots, reserved for hardware SHIFT functions. —

A slow compressor (2.5:1 above about −14 dBFS) glues the mix after TILT, and a limiter after VOLUME stops clipping. If the PEAK reading on the screen sits near 0 dB for long, turn VOLUME or DRIVE down.

Motion: the tides

Tides are four slow modulators that move one control each, continuously, whatever the autopilot is set to. They are how you build motion you choose yourself.

Tide Shape Default destination Good for
T1 SINE ALL CHAR 2 Smooth breathing of every engine's second character control
T2 TRIANGLE B TONE Steady filter sweeps that turn round at the ends
T3 RANDOM GRAIN A new random target each cycle, glided to smoothly
T4 DRIFT SHIMMER Wandering, never-repeating movement

The MOTION page bank holds each tide's two knobs:

  • RATE: the length of one cycle, from 0.5 s to 15 minutes. Drones usually want 30 s or slower.
  • DEPTH: how far it moves the destination, centred on 0. Right of centre pushes the control up first; left of centre is the same movement inverted. At the extremes a tide swings its destination by half the control's full range either side.

Under the bank, each tide has a live scope and a destination menu with 34 choices:

  • One voice: A–D CHAR 1, CHAR 2, TONE, LEVEL or SPACE. Character controls are listed by the current engine's names (for example "A · DRIVE").
  • All voices: ALL CHAR 1, ALL CHAR 2, ALL TONE, ALL SPACE.
  • Effects: CLOUD, GRAIN, SCATTER, DELAY, REVERB, SHIMMER, DAMPING, WOW, TILT.
  • OFF.

A tide adds to the control's setting and the autopilot's drift, and the yellow dot on the knob shows the combined result. Locking a knob also blocks tides from it.

The autopilot

The AUTOPILOT dial sets how far the machine may stray from your settings; the switches on the AUTOPILOT page set where it may stray. At 0% Undertow is a plain instrument; at 100% it composes on its own. Your knob settings stay the 'home' it keeps returning to.

What each level unlocks

Each level adds to everything below it. The tick marks around the dial sit at these points.

From Name What starts happening Needs switch
5% DRIFT Every knob with drift wanders slowly around your setting; more dial = wider wander. TIMBRE
25% HARMONY The harmonic centre walks around the scale. HARMONY
25% (breathing) Voices fade out and back in; DENSITY sets how many sound at once. BREATHING
40% SURGE A 10–30 s swell opens every voice's TONE, raises sends, cloud and drive. About 3 in 10 are ebbs instead: everything darkens. TIMBRE
50% VOICING Voices move onto chord tones of the new centre. The cloud freezes for 6–30 s or changes grain pitch. HARMONY / TIMBRE
60% MUTATE The whole patch morphs to a nearby variation of itself. TIMBRE
75% ENGINES A voice fades out and returns as a different engine; slow morphs toward your stored scenes. ENGINES & SCENES
90% KEY The root moves up a fourth or a fifth. KEY CHANGES (off by default)

The autopilot makes one move at a time. The countdown under the dial shows when the next one comes; NUDGE makes it now. Every move is written to the screen and to the LOG on the AUTOPILOT page, with the time since START.

The dedicated knobs

Knob What it does Range
PACE Average time between moves; also how fast drift wanders. about 160 s (slow) to 9 s (fast)
DENSITY How many enabled voices the breathing keeps on at once (left = 1, right = all). Also thickens the cloud. 0–100%
MORPH Length of scene and seed morphs. Autopilot mutations take 2.5× this, scene walks 3×. 0.5 s–3 min
VOLUME Master output level, before the limiter. 0–100%

These four, AUTOPILOT and the switches are not stored in scenes, so a morph never changes how the machine behaves.

Keeping control

  • Touching a knob holds the autopilot off it for 6 s, so your move is heard as you made it.
  • The padlock under a knob (or L on a focused knob) locks it for good: no drift, no tides, no mutation, and a locked ENGINE is never swapped. Locks are saved with the patch.
  • The switches remove a whole kind of move. For example, HARMONY off keeps every note where you put it while the sound still evolves.
  • Voice on/off wins over breathing: a voice you switched off never breathes back in.

Scenes, seeds, files and recording

Everything here is on the PATCH page, except recording, which is REC in the header.

Scenes

Eight slots, each holding a complete sound: engines, notes, knobs, tides and effects. Each filled slot shows four coloured bars (lit = voice on), the key and scale, and the first three letters of each voice's engine.

  1. Store: press STORE, then tap a slot. The slot outlines turn dashed while STORE is armed.
  2. Recall: with MORPH TO selected (the normal mode), tap a filled slot. The whole machine glides there over the MORPH time. Stepped controls (engines, notes, scale) switch halfway through the morph.
  3. Clear: press CLEAR, then tap the slot.

With the autopilot at 75% or more and ENGINES & SCENES on, it also drifts between your stored scenes on its own. Storing three or four contrasting scenes is the easiest way to give it a 'piece' to wander through.

Seeds

Every generated patch comes from a number. The same seed always builds the same patch, so a seed is a short way to remember or share a sound.

  • NEW SEED: picks a random number and builds its patch now.
  • GENERATE: builds the patch for the number in the box.
  • MORPH TO SEED: glides to that patch over the MORPH time instead of jumping.

Generated patches favour dark modes (Aeolian, Dorian, Phrygian) and put low engines on A, body engines on B, upper or textural engines on C and D.

Files

  • EXPORT FILE: saves the patch, locks and all eight scenes as a .json file (you confirm the save). Where saving isn't possible, the text appears in the box below instead.
  • COPY TEXT: copies the same text to the clipboard.
  • LOAD TEXT: paste an exported patch into the box, then press it.
  • RESET ALL: returns to the default patch and clears locks (tap twice to confirm). Scenes are kept.

The current patch, locks, scenes and seed are also saved automatically in your browser, so the machine reopens as you left it on the same device.

Recording

Press REC to record exactly what you hear; the timer shows the length. Press STOP REC to finish. A player appears under the header, and you are asked whether to save the file (.webm, or .mp4 in Safari, at 256 kbps). SAVE FILE offers it again if you declined. Pressing STOP on the engine also ends the recording.

Troubleshooting

Problem Likely cause Fix
No sound at all The engine has not started, or the device is muted Press START (the chip should show a sample rate such as 48.0 kHz); check system volume and VOLUME. On iPhone, also check the silent switch.
An error under START The browser lacks AudioWorklet support Use a current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari and reload the page.
One voice is silent Switched off, breathing out, or TONE below its pitch Check the tile's status and power square; raise TONE above the note's frequency shown on the tile; raise LEVEL.
A voice is too deep to hear on a phone speaker C1 range sits at 33–65 Hz, below what small speakers play Use headphones, or raise OCTAVE, or use TITAN/WALL whose harmonics carry the note.
Harsh or distorted DRIVE high, WALL RESONANCE near 100%, or METAL ringing hard Lower DRIVE, RESONANCE or VOLUME; raise DAMPING; the limiter only catches peaks.
Nothing ever changes AUTOPILOT under 5%, switches off, or knobs locked Raise AUTOPILOT; check the AUTOPILOT page switches and padlocks; or add a tide.
Too busy or restless PACE fast, AUTOPILOT high Lower PACE, set AUTOPILOT to 25–40%, lock what must stay, turn HARMONY off to freeze the notes.
Notes wander off where I set them HARMONY autopilot moving the centre and voicing Turn the HARMONY switch off, or keep AUTOPILOT below 25%.
Sound stops when the phone locks The phone suspends the browser Keep the screen on (Undertow asks the browser to stay awake while playing) or plug in power.
Settings gone after reopening Private browsing, cleared site data, or another device Export patches you care about with EXPORT FILE and load them with LOAD TEXT.
Version 1 patch will not load The control set changed in version 2 Rebuild it by ear; v1 and v2 patches are not compatible.
'Saving is not available here' The page is open outside claude.ai Play the recording back in the page player, or open Undertow from claude.ai to save files.
Log says 'Signal fault: engines reset' A rare numerical overload Nothing to do: the engines reset themselves silently and carry on.
